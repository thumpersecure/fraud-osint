The Fraud-OSINT Wiki is an educational reference on forensic accounting and open-source intelligence. This page explains where its content comes from, how it was assembled, and how far it should be trusted.

1 Origin

This wiki began as a request to read seven PDF documents on forensic accounting and OSINT, summarize them, and turn the findings into a structured, interlinked reference. The source documents span two full professional textbooks, two academic papers, a national-firm training deck, one chapter of a European law-enforcement course, and an anti-corruption quick guide — about 1,142 pages in total.

3 Method

The seven documents were read in full and reduced to structured summaries. Their bibliographies were harvested and key citations verified. Four topic-focused research passes then confirmed and refreshed the statistics, tool inventories, legal standards, and case facts. The resulting material was reorganized — not merely concatenated — into encyclopedic articles that cross-reference one another. Where the original documents disagreed with current fact (for example, a tracing tool that has since shut down, or a reporting rule that has since changed), the current position is stated and flagged.

4 Design philosophy

The presentation deliberately imitates the early-2000s MediaWiki "Monobook" look: a serif masthead, a left navigation column, a gold content rule, and the classic blue links. This is not only nostalgia. Each page is a few kilobytes of plain HTML with a single shared stylesheet, no web fonts, no images, no analytics, and no JavaScript required to read. It loads instantly on slow or metered connections — the way reference material on the early web did.

5 Caveats and limitations

This is an educational synthesis, not legal, financial, or operational advice. Laws, thresholds, and tools change; figures are point-in-time. Before relying on anything here for a real investigation, engagement, or legal proceeding, verify it against the cited primary sources and qualified professionals. Nothing here should be used to target private individuals or to conduct unauthorized access; see OSINT Ethics.

Coverage is broad but not exhaustive, and the wiki inherits the emphases of its sources (U.S. and EU practice; occupational and financial-statement fraud; trafficking and elder-abuse applications). Contributions from primary sources would strengthen weaker articles.
