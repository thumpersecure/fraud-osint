The Fraud-OSINT Wiki is an encyclopedic handbook covering how financial crime is detected, investigated, proven in court, and traced across the open internet. It joins two disciplines that increasingly work together: forensic accounting — the application of accounting and investigation to matters of law — and open-source intelligence — the systematic use of publicly available information. The forensic-accounting material supplies the how to prove it; the OSINT material supplies the how to find it.

Three ideas run through nearly every article: follow the money, because fraud leaves a financial trail; evidence is only useful if it is admissible, so method reliability and chain of custody matter as much as the finding; and tools assist but never replace human judgment.

Did you know…

  • …that organizations lose an estimated 5% of revenue to occupational fraud each year, with a median loss of $145,000 per case?[1]
  • …that tips catch more fraud (43%) than audits, controls, and management review combined?[1]
  • …that Al Capone was convicted not for bootlegging but for tax evasion, using the net-worth method still used today?
  • …that in Benford's Law, the digit 1 leads about 30% of natural numbers — and deviations can expose fabricated data?
  • …that in August 2026 the U.S. permanently ended beneficial-ownership reporting for domestic companies under the Corporate Transparency Act?[2]
